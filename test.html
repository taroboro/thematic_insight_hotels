Post-Stanford Hotel Observations — Thematic Clusters

Field observations at Raffles Hotel and The Fullerton Hotel during peak check-in periods

1
Physical Labour Intensity
Multiple bellboys per heavy luggage car Bellboys drag luggage to central point Trolleys not brought to vehicles Manual tagging with pen and paper Varied luggage types require individual judgement Oversized and oddly shaped luggage Plastic bags and loose souvenirs alongside formal luggage Bellboy assists guest into wheelchair from vehicle Heavy items require separate trolley deployment
2
Spatial Congestion & Visual Clutter
Luggage carts pulled diagonally across lobby Carts cut through guest circulation paths Loose bags placed on lobby floors by guests Groups clustering near entrance and seating Check-in queues lengthen after 2:30pm 15–20 min average check-in wait Covered trolleys obscure luggage contents LV bag and personal items placed on ground Luxury feel diminished by crowding and clutter
3
Guest Autonomy in Luggage Handling
~1 in 5 guests prefer to self-carry Guests carry 3–4 pieces themselves Guests retain personal bags while bellboy handles rest Fullerton: guest declined bellboy for 3 pieces Guest unaware bellboy was offering help Guests with 1–2 items typically self-carry Motivation unclear — security, time, or convenience? Inconsistent handoff between guest and bellboy
4
Service Coverage Inconsistency
Some guests unattended on arrival Groups sit and wait without clear guidance Service driven by staff availability, not standard Fullerton: only 3 staff outside during visit Bellboys assist checkout guests, splitting attention Guest relation agents also manage front desk Vehicle priority system observed (limousines first) Manager handles disputes; bellboy handles luggage only
